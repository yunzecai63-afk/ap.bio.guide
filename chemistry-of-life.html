Water: the molecule that makes life possible

Why water is polar

  • Oxygen is more electronegative than hydrogen.
  • Electrons are shared unequally in water.
  • Oxygen becomes slightly negative, and hydrogens become slightly positive.
  • This polarity allows hydrogen bonding between water molecules.
Water’s polarity is the reason it has unusual life-supporting properties.

Water polarity

Key properties of water

  • Cohesion: water sticks to water
  • Adhesion: water sticks to other polar surfaces
  • High specific heat: resists temperature change
  • Heat of vaporization: evaporation cools surfaces
  • Excellent solvent: surrounds ions and polar molecules
  • Ice is less dense than liquid water: important for aquatic life

Macromolecules

AP Bio students should know the major biological macromolecules, what they are made of, what they do, and the key structural features that appear again and again.

Carbohydrates

  • Monomers: monosaccharides
  • Examples: glucose, fructose
  • Polymers: polysaccharides
  • Storage: starch in plants, glycogen in animals
  • Structure: cellulose in plant cell walls
  • AP structure to remember: many hydroxyl groups (–OH)
Carbohydrates are often rich in hydroxyl groups, which makes many of them polar.

Lipids

  • Hydrophobic and mostly nonpolar
  • Examples: fats, phospholipids, steroids
  • Triglyceride = glycerol + 3 fatty acids
  • Phospholipids build cell membranes
  • AP structure to remember: long hydrocarbon chains
  • Important feature: ester linkage in fats and phosphate group in phospholipids
Lipids store long-term energy and are less polar than carbohydrates.

Proteins

  • Monomers: amino acids
  • Joined by peptide bonds
  • Functions: enzymes, structure, transport, signaling, defense
  • AP structure to remember: amino group (–NH₂), carboxyl group (–COOH), and R group
  • Protein shape determines function
Every amino acid has the same basic structure, but the R group changes from one amino acid to another.

Nucleic Acids

  • Monomers: nucleotides
  • DNA stores genetic information
  • RNA helps express genetic information
  • AP structure to remember: phosphate group, 5-carbon sugar, nitrogenous base
  • DNA uses A, T, C, G; RNA uses A, U, C, G
Nucleic acids are about information storage and transfer.

Building and breaking molecules

Dehydration synthesis

Builds larger molecules by removing water.

Monomer + monomer → polymer + H₂O

Hydrolysis

Breaks larger molecules apart by adding water.

Polymer + H₂O → smaller molecules

Quick review

Must-know ideas for Unit 1

  • Water is polar, so it forms hydrogen bonds.
  • Hydrogen bonding explains cohesion, adhesion, temperature stability, and solvent properties.
  • Carbohydrates store energy and provide structure.
  • Lipids store long-term energy and form membranes.
  • Proteins are made of amino acids and depend on shape.
  • Nucleic acids store and transmit genetic information.
  • Dehydration builds polymers; hydrolysis breaks them apart.
